# Scientific Hypotheses: CoralTwin-DT

## Primary Overarching Hypothesis ($H_1$)

> **La aplicación de un gemelo digital basado en inteligencia artificial y datos ambientales permite identificar áreas prioritarias de restauración coralina y mejorar la planificación de conservación frente al cambio climático.**
>
> *(The deployment of an environmental digital twin coupling artificial intelligence and multi-source oceanographic data enables high-fidelity identification of priority coral restoration zones and optimizes spatial conservation planning under accelerated climate change).*

---

## Working Sub-Hypotheses ($H_{1.1} - H_{1.4}$)

### Sub-Hypothesis 1: Compound Synergistic Stress ($H_{1.1}$)
The compounding effect of thermal stress ($DHW \ge 8^\circ\text{C-weeks}$) and depressed aragonite saturation ($\Omega_{\text{arag}} \le 3.0$) lowers the thermal bleaching threshold by $\ge 1.2^\circ\text{C-weeks}$ relative to baseline alkaline conditions, inducing an accelerated state transition towards macroalgal dominance.

### Sub-Hypothesis 2: Non-Linear Predictive Superiority ($H_{1.2}$)
Nonlinear gradient-boosted decision architectures (XGBoost) and deep multi-layer perceptrons (MLP) achieve significantly higher predictive skill ($F_1 > 0.90$, $R^2 > 0.85$) than traditional linear biophysical models by capturing multi-scale interactions between satellite-derived turbidity ($K_d$), sea surface temperature anomalies ($SSTA$), and localized benthic rugosity.

### Sub-Hypothesis 3: Intervention Synergy ($H_{1.3}$)
Neither active coral restoration (outplanting thermally tolerant micro-fragments) nor passive conservation (MPA herbivory enforcement) alone is sufficient to maintain $>30\%$ live coral cover under SSP5-8.5 by 2050; only a combined hybrid intervention strategy achieves sustainable net-positive calcification ($G_{\text{net}} > 0$).

### Sub-Hypothesis 4: Spatial Refugia Optimization ($H_{1.4}$)
Prioritizing spatial restoration in micro-refugia characterized by high hydrodynamic flushing and moderate natural turbidity increases 10-year post-outplanting coral survival by $\ge 42\%$ compared to random or uniform distribution strategies.
